# Voiceover — ~100s, ~165 wpm

Read flat and unhurried. Every claim below is one the footage proves.

---

**[0:00 — cold open, over the FluxA approval and the ticket number]**

> A hundred and seven dollars, authorised by a human. Then a real ticket.
> Nothing in between happened without permission.

**[0:08 — title]**

> Flight Desk is an AI agent that books flights based on your schedule — and
> pays under your permission.

**[0:16 — the mystery box]**

> You do not give it a route. You give it a constraint.
> Two hundred dollars, two days, somewhere to watch the sunrise, out of Hong Kong.

**[0:26 — searching]**

> It picks the destinations itself — Taipei for Alishan's cloud sea, Da Nang for
> an east-facing beach — and searches live Atlas inventory for each.

**[0:40 — comparison]**

> Then it does the part a search box cannot. It holds the budget as a ceiling
> and names what it rules out. It compares two fares on what actually decides
> the choice — seven dollars more, six fewer hours on the ground.

**[0:55 — self-fill and draft]**

> Passenger details are already on file, so it fills them in. The passport
> number never enters the agent's context — the server expands it on the way to
> the airline.

**[1:04 — the gate]**

> It prepares the booking. It stops there.

**[1:06 — THE SPINE]**

> Now the part that matters. Most Atlas fares take no card at all, so this one
> settles from an agency deposit — and that needs your signature.
>
> FluxA raises a mandate for the exact amount, for this booking.
> You approve it. Not the agent — you.
>
> And the agent picks the task back up on its own.

**[1:26 — architecture]**

> Atlas provides the inventory, the fares and the ticketing.
> FluxA holds the money.
> The agent has no tool to place, pay or refund. Not disabled — absent.

**[1:34 — close]**

> Ticketed. From a sentence to a boarding pass, with every dollar under human
> control.
